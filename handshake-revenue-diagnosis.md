## What was submitted
Price-volume bridge, FY2025 vs FY2024, U.S. theatres. Revenue = Attendance x (Average ticket + Food & beverage / concession per patron). Volume effect = change in attendance x 2024 spend per visit. Spend effect = 2025 attendance x change in spend per visit.

- AMC: attendance 156.866m to 155.810m (-1.056m, -0.7%); avg ticket $12.22 to $12.78 (+$0.56); food & beverage per patron $8.30 to $8.57 (+$0.27); spend per visit $20.52 to $21.35 (+$0.83, +4.0%); modelled admissions + food & beverage $3,218.9m to $3,326.5m (+$107.7m, +3.3%); volume -$21.7m, spend +$129.3m (ticket +$87.3m; food & beverage +$42.1m).
- Cinemark: attendance 122.9m to 120.3m (-2.6m, -2.1%); avg ticket $10.03 to $10.52 (+$0.49); concession per patron $7.89 to $8.30 (+$0.41); spend per visit $17.92 to $18.82 (+$0.90, +5.0%); modelled admissions + concession $2,202.4m to $2,264.0m (+$61.7m, +2.8%); volume -$46.6m, spend +$108.3m (ticket +$58.9m; concession +$49.3m).
- Key comparison: AMC lost $24.9m less revenue to falling visits than Cinemark (-$21.7m vs -$46.6m). Cinemark grew spend per visit faster (+5.0% vs +4.0%).
- Takeaways: price and concessions, not footfall, drove 2025 growth; AMC's advantage was retention; Cinemark's per-visit growth was stronger; geography matters (worldwide AMC volume -$88.6m, spend +$228.2m; Cinemark worldwide -$109.6m volume, +$160.2m spend); A Minecraft Movie was No. 1 domestic film of 2025 at $423.9m, just ahead of Lilo & Stitch at $423.8m — context, not a cause.
- Reconciliation: Cinemark reported U.S. segment admissions + concession $2,264.2m vs $2,202.4m (+$61.8m) vs model $2,264.0m (+$61.7m), a $0.1m rounding difference. Scope is admissions + concessions only, not total revenue (AMC total $4,848.9m vs $4,637.2m, +$211.7m; Cinemark $3,115.0m vs $3,049.5m, +$65.5m).

All figures verified directly against those releases.
