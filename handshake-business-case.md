## What was submitted
One-slide Google Slides answer (title + issue tree + what each branch showed), built from the mission's Converse case and fact file, framed as a data-driven analysis using Teja's resume (MS Computer Science, UNT, GPA 4.0; Software Engineer at Foot Locker, Summer 2026; MLOps/Data Engineering at TCS; creator of ZHuddle Python learning platform).

Answer: Converse revenue fell 28% ($366M to $263M, Jun-Aug 2026) because pairs sold fell 34% while average price rose — a demand collapse, not a pricing problem. Price ruled out (average price rose on richer mix and less discounting); wholesale fell 25% and direct sales 34%; marketing spend fell 58% ($33M to $14M); not a category slump (Nike brand +2% pairs in North America, On +12.4% in the Americas, Vans only -9.8%). Next step: one-region Chuck Taylor relaunch, tracking weekly pairs sold against a control region.
